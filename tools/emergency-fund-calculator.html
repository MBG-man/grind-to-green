How the emergency fund calculator works

Your target is based on the cost of covering essential bills for the number of months you select. Your savings gap is the target minus your existing emergency savings. The monthly amount needed divides that gap by your selected deadline, while the time estimate uses the monthly amount you can currently save.

Emergency fund target = essential monthly expenses × months of coverage.

Use essential costs rather than your full lifestyle budget. Leave out optional shopping, entertainment and planned purchases. Revisit the calculation after major changes to your income, household or regular bills.

Frequently asked questions

How many months should my emergency fund cover?

Three to six months of essential expenses is a common starting range. A larger buffer may make sense for freelancers, people with variable income, single-income households or anyone who may need longer to replace their income.

What counts as an essential expense?

Include housing, basic groceries, utilities, essential transport, insurance, phone service and minimum debt payments. Exclude discretionary spending and costs you can plan for separately.

Does this calculator include interest?

No. It uses a simple savings plan and assumes no interest, fees or inflation. Actual account interest may change your results slightly.

What if the monthly amount needed is too high?

Choose a longer deadline or start with a smaller milestone, such as one month of essential expenses. A manageable regular contribution is better than a plan that puts other necessary bills at risk.

Should I invest my emergency fund?

Usually not. Emergency savings should be accessible and relatively stable. Stocks and crypto can lose value when you need to withdraw, so keep investing separate from your emergency reserve.
